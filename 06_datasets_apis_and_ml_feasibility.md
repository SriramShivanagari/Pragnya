## 2. Ten Essential Scientific Considerations (Section 8 Requirement)

1. **Are sufficient historical crop yield labels available?**
   **Yes at the district level ([UPAg](https://upag.gov.in/), [ICRISAT](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/ywp3y5j9vv/1)); No at the individual plot level.** Therefore, training a supervised regressor directly on district labels and applying a bounded, physically grounded **Plot-to-District `SAVI/NDMI` Anomaly Ratio** is the only scientifically honest approach.
2. **Do satellite observations correspond to the actual crop growth period?**
   Full-year satellite averages include fallow summer months and corrupt ML models. We filter satellite observations strictly between `Sowing_Date` and `Sowing_Date + Crop_Duration` (e.g., Day 0 to Day 81–120 for Kharif Paddy) and extract phenological milestones (`Peak SAVI`, `Day-81 SAVI`, `Integral under SAVI curve`, `Flowering-Stage NDMI`).
3. **How does cloud cover affect optical imagery?**
   During India's June–September southwest monsoon and October–November cyclones, optical cloud cover invalidates 40–70% of Sentinel-2 passes. We filter pixels using the Sentinel-2 Scene Classification (`SCL`) band, apply **Savitzky-Golay temporal smoothing**, and impute gaps >10 days using **Sentinel-1 C-band SAR (`VH/VV` and `RVI`)** ([MDPI 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/17/8/1412)).
4. **Do different crops need different models?**
   **Yes.** A paddy field intentionally flooded at transplantation has a completely different `NDMI` and `SAR VV` signature than rainfed cotton or Rabi wheat. We train **crop-specific model heads** (focusing on **Paddy, Wheat, Cotton, and Maize** for the 15-day MVP) with crop-specific FAO-56 $K_c$ and $K_y$ parameters.
5. **How does regional variation affect prediction accuracy?**
   A model trained on alluvial high-input Punjab districts (`~40–45 qtl/ha` paddy) will severely overestimate yields in rainfed Odisha or Telangana unless agro-climatic zone embeddings and historical district yield priors ($\bar{Y}_{d, \text{5yr}}$) are included as baseline features.
6. **Does available data genuinely support field-level predictions?**
   Sentinel-2 (10m) and Sentinel-1 (10m) provide ~40 pixels per 1-acre plot (4,046 $\text{m}^2$). To prevent boundary road/tree contamination ("mixed pixels"), we apply a **10m inward morphological buffer** on the plot polygon before computing median index values.
7. **How are missing inputs handled?**
   Through **Hierarchical Spatial Imputation + Conformal Interval Widening**: if a farmer does not enter lab soil values, the system falls back to `Village SHC Mean -> SoilGrids 250m -> District Prior`, and automatically widens the `P10–P90` prediction interval to reflect increased epistemic uncertainty.
8. **How do we prevent data leakage during training?**
   **Strict Temporal Out-of-Time Split + Spatial GroupKFold:** Never use random `train_test_split` on agricultural panel data (which leaks shared year-wide monsoon anomalies across neighboring districts). We train on years **1998–2022**, validate on **2023**, and hold out **2024–2025** as an unseen temporal test set, plus evaluate unseen districts via `GroupKFold(groups=district_id)`.
9. **How do we measure prediction uncertainty?**
   Using **Split Conformal Quantile Regression (CQR)** with pinball loss ($\alpha \in \{0.10, 0.50, 0.90\}$), calibrated on the validation year so that the empirical coverage of `[P10, P90]` is guaranteed to be $\ge 80\%$ on held-out data.
10. **How do we evaluate recommendations independently of yield predictions?**
    We evaluate the **Counterfactual Action Engine** against three agronomic verification tests:
    * *Leaching Avoidance Rate:* % of simulated heavy-rain events (>25mm in 48h) where the optimizer successfully delays urea application.
    * *Water Use Efficiency (`kg yield / m³ water`):* Reduction in irrigation volume vs. fixed 7-day flood schedules under FAO-56 soil-water balance backtesting.
    * *Zero-Violation Rate on ICAR Nutrient Caps:* 100% compliance with maximum safe seasonal N-P-K thresholds.

## 3. AI/ML Model Investigation & Selection (Section 8 Requirement)

| Model Architecture | Suitability for Our Problem | Data Required | Strengths | Weaknesses | Compute & Training Complexity | Role in **Pragnya KisanPulse** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ridge / ElasticNet Linear Regression** | Baseline benchmark only | Tabular seasonal aggregates | Fast, interpretable coefficients, zero overfitting on small panels | Cannot capture non-linear heat-stress thresholds (>35°C) or waterlogging penalties | <1 second on CPU | **Baseline Benchmark** to prove ML uplift |
| **Random Forest Regressor** | Moderate | Tabular weather + soil + spectral summary features | Robust to outliers and non-linear interactions | Cannot extrapolate trends; slow inference for quantile ensembles; poor handling of high-cardinality district IDs | ~15 seconds on CPU | Secondary baseline |
| **CatBoost / XGBoost with Quantile Loss** | **Primary Production Model** | Tabular + engineered temporal features (`SAVI_day81`, `GDD_flowering`, `NDMI_min`, `SHC_SOC`, `District_Prior`) | State-of-the-art on tabular/heterogeneous data; native handling of missing values (`NaN`) and categorical agro-zones; built-in `MultiQuantile` loss + exact TreeSHAP explanations | Requires explicit temporal feature engineering (`SAVI` slope, integral, stage-wise rain) | **~20–45 seconds on standard laptop CPU** | **Core Stage-1 & Stage-2 Yield + Uncertainty Engine** |
| **Bi-LSTM / 1D Temporal CNN** | Moderate-High for sequential phenology | Regular 5-day time-series tensor (`[T=24 steps × 8 bands/weather vars]`) | Learns sequential dependencies across growth stages ([ResearchGate Oct 2026](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/417267952_Hybrid_Machine_Learning_Models_for_Agricultural_Yield_Prediction_Integrating_Satellite_Imagery_Weather_Data_and_Soil_Parameters_for_Indian_Crops)) | Overfits when trained on <10,000 district-year labels; opaque to explain; sensitive to irregular cloud gaps | 5–15 minutes on GPU/CPU | Used as a **Lightweight Temporal Feature Extractor** (or compared in ablation study) |
| **2D/3D Vision CNN / ViT on Raw Satellite Patches** | **Rejected for 15-Day MVP** | Terabytes of raw multi-spectral GeoTIFF chips paired with plot-level yield polygons | Captures spatial texture within large fields | **Infeasible in 15 days:** India lacks open plot-level yield polygon labels to train a 2D CNN without massive overfitting | Hours/days on multi-GPU + TB storage | **Excluded** (Zonal statistics + `SAVI/NDMI/NDRE/RVI` time-series beat raw CNNs on small/medium plots) |
| **Hybrid Crop-Physics (FAO-56 / Aquacrop Rules) + ML Residual** | **Primary Optimization Engine** | Daily weather ($ET_0$, Rain), Soil texture ($\theta_{\text{FC}}, \theta_{\text{WP}}$), Crop $K_c, K_y$, ML baseline yield | Physically consistent counterfactuals: guaranteed never to predict that "0 water = maximum yield" or "infinite urea = infinite yield" | Requires crop-specific $K_c$ and $K_y$ lookup tables | <10 milliseconds per 7-day simulation | **Core Counterfactual Action Optimizer** |

### Evaluation Metrics We Will Report in the Prototype
* **Point Accuracy (Out-of-Time 2024–2025 Test Split):** Mean Absolute Error (**MAE** in quintals/ha), Root Mean Squared Error (**RMSE**), Mean Absolute Percentage Error (**MAPE %**), and Coefficient of Determination (**$R^2$**).
* **Uncertainty Calibration:** **Prediction Interval Coverage Probability (PICP)** for the `P10–P90` interval (target: $\approx 80\%$) and **Mean Prediction Interval Width (MPIW)**.
* *Strict Honesty Rule:* We do **not** claim a fabricated accuracy number (like "98.5% accuracy") before running our experimental pipeline on Day 7–8; in the pitch deck, we state our literature-backed target benchmark (`MAPE < 8–12%` at Day 81 per [IJISAE](https://ijisae.org/index.php/IJISAE/article/view/2326) and [Global Agri Vision 2026](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404936058_Global_Agri_Vision_Artificial_Intelligence_Transforming_Agronomy_with_Predictive_Analytics)) and show our live backtest validator.
